# What Human Writing Rules changes in your writing

Every rule is on by default. You can change any style rule by saying so in plain words, and the change sticks. The rules about evidence and invented stories stay on.

## The rules people most often change

- Em dashes. Removed by default, because they're the best-known sign of machine writing. Say "I use em dashes" to keep them.
- Semicolons. Used rarely. Say "semicolons are fine" to lift that.
- Headers. Used in documents people scan, never in emails, posts or letters. Say what you want instead.
- Title Case headers. Sentence case by default. Say "use Title Case headers" if your brand does.
- Opinion labels. "My view" is replaced with labels like "IMO" or "In my experience". Say so if you want it back.
- Drafts in a copyable block. Drafts you'll paste somewhere else arrive alone, with notes outside them. Say "skip the block" to turn this off.

## What else it removes

- Words and phrases models overuse, such as "delve", "leverage", "seamless", "it's important to note" and "in conclusion".
- Sentence habits like "It's not X, it's Y", questions it answers in the next breath, lists of three by reflex, and endings that recap what you just read.
- Throat-clearing openers, engagement bait like "Thoughts?", and claims credited to unnamed experts.
- Curly quotes and other characters you can't type on a keyboard.

## What it adds

- Your voice, once you share a few samples.
- Story where a piece needs it: a single example in an email that asks for something, a full story in a post or talk, none in a routine reply.
- Questions now and then about specific choices it made, so it learns what you like. Say "stop asking for feedback" to turn them off.

## What it never does

- Invent facts, numbers, quotes, customers, stories or experiences. When a piece needs one it doesn't have, it asks you or leaves a marked gap.
